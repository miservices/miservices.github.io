Travel Information

Getting around Michigan, mile by mile.

Whether you're new to Michigan roads or just passing through Flint, here's what to know about getting around: public transit, rest stops, truck travel, winter driving, and the rules of the road.

01

Public transit

MDOT doesn't run local bus routes directly, but it helps fund and coordinate transit across the state — connecting Michigan's cities through intercity bus service and supporting local transit agencies that run day-to-day routes in their communities.

In Flint, local bus service is run separately from MDOT by the city's own transit agency. If you're new to the area, check their site directly for routes, schedules, and fares before you head out.

02

Rest areas & amenities

MDOT maintains rest areas and welcome centers along Michigan's highways, and we're steadily modernizing them with better lighting, accessible restrooms, pet exercise areas, vending, and EV charging where it makes sense.

If you're driving long distances, especially in winter, plan your stops — rest area spacing can be wider in less populated stretches, so don't wait until you're on empty to look for one.

03

Truck travel

Commercial trucks share the same highways as everyone else, so knowing the basics helps traffic move safely for all drivers.

  • Stay out of a truck's blind spots — if you can't see the driver's mirrors, they likely can't see you.
  • Give trucks extra room to brake and turn; they need more distance than passenger vehicles.
  • Oversize and overweight loads require permits — see Permits & Licensing for details if you're hauling freight through Michigan.
  • Watch for designated truck routes and posted weight limits on local roads and bridges.
04

Winter driving

Michigan winters are no joke, and roads can change condition fast — especially if you're used to driving somewhere milder.

  • Bridges freeze before roads do. Even when the pavement looks clear, bridge decks ice over first — slow down as you approach.
  • Lake-effect snow can drop visibility to near zero in bands that move through quickly. If you hit one, slow down and turn on your headlights.
  • Keep extra following distance — stopping takes longer on snow and ice.
  • Give snowplows room to work. Don't pass on the right, and stay back — plows throw snow and ice.
  • Keep a winter emergency kit in your car: blanket, flashlight, phone charger, and a few basics in case you're stuck waiting out a storm.

Know before you go

Check conditions before a winter trip — road conditions can shift from clear to hazardous in a short drive, especially near the Great Lakes.

05

Speed limits & the rules of the road

Posted speed limits vary by road type, so pay attention to signage — especially when moving between freeways and local streets.

Road type Typical limit
Rural freeways Up to 75 mph
Urban freeways 65–70 mph
Rural highways 55 mph
City & residential streets 25–35 mph
Work zones Reduced — fines double

A few things that catch newcomers off guard:

  • The "Michigan Left." On many divided highways, you can't turn directly left at an intersection. Instead, continue past it, use the median crossover to U-turn, then turn right. It looks strange the first time, but it keeps traffic moving.
  • Move Over law. If you see a stopped police car, tow truck, or road crew with flashing lights, move over a lane if you can, or slow down.
  • Deer crossings. Michigan sees a lot of deer-vehicle collisions, especially around dawn, dusk, and fall. Stay alert on rural roads and near wooded areas.
  • Right lane laws. On multi-lane freeways, slower traffic should generally keep right except when passing.
